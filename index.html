04 / HOW THE MODEL WORKS

Count. Decide. Switch safely.

A simple, testable rule that can be explained in your presentation.

01

COUNT

Estimate vehicle arrivals and track the queue waiting on each approach.

Input: arrivals + queues
02

DECIDE

Start with a base green time, then distribute remaining time based on queue size. Keep green between 12 and 32 seconds.

Goal: reduce average wait
03

SWITCH

End green, allow yellow and clearance time, then serve the next road. Real systems need certified safety controls.

4s yellow + 1s clearance
THE ADAPTIVE RULE

More waiting vehicles → a larger share of green time

The rule recalculates at the start of each model cycle. It does not connect to real cameras or signals.

12s ≤ green ≤ 32s

05 / IMPACT & NEXT STEPS

From school model to safe pilot.

Use the model to explore whether adaptive timing is worth testing before any real junction is changed.

◷

Less waiting

Compare average wait and longest queue to check how effectively green time is used.

↗

Smoother flow

When traffic is uneven, adaptive timing can move green time toward the approaches that need it.

♧

Fuel & pollution

Less idling may reduce fuel use and emissions, but this prototype does not directly measure them.

WHAT TRAFFIC POLICE COULD TEST

A cautious, data-led pilot

  1. Collect approved traffic counts from safe locations and repeat observations.
  2. Run fixed and adaptive methods using the same traffic inputs.
  3. Review safety, pedestrians, emergency vehicles and costs with traffic engineers.
  4. Consider a supervised real-world trial only after formal approval.
